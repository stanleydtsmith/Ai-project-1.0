Lawn Care Advice

A few things worth knowing

Some notes from our team on keeping a lawn healthy between visits.

Mowing regularly — weekly or fortnightly — keeps a lawn in good health through spring and summer. Vary the cutting height and frequency through the season, never cut too short, and avoid letting growth exceed around 1.5". Try mowing in different directions each time, so the grass isn't always pushed the same way. Rotary mowers suit most domestic lawns; cylinder mowers suit flatter, finer surfaces.

Moss thrives in damp, poorly drained lawns, and winter is the best time to tackle it. Sulphate of iron applied over the winter months helps, and if the weather allows, late February is a good window for scarifying.

Feeding is the most important part of the lawn care cycle — giving the grass the nutrients it needs to kick-start growth in spring. A slow- or controlled-release granular fertiliser gives the lawn the best possible start.

Lawns naturally dry out through summer. If you water, do it properly — a good soak for an hour or two, rather than a light daily sprinkle — so the lawn holds onto the work done through the year. Choosing not to water? Simply mow less often, and on a higher setting.

Scarification is an essential part of cultured turf management, carried out with a petrol-driven scarifier. It encourages healthy, vertical growth of the desirable grasses and improves the surface and appearance of the turf. A very light version — a drag brush or rake on a cylinder mower — simply raises the leaves before mowing for a smarter finish. Considering scarification in Suffolk or North Essex? Get in touch.
